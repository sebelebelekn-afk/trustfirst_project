You must be 18

Unlike coins, a wallet balance is real money, and it is yours. It is kept separate from coins and shown separately in the app.

If something went wrong: a payment taken twice, coins that never arrived, a gift the app sent by mistake. Write to contacttrustfirst@gmail.com and we will put it right. This is separate from changing your mind, and there is no time limit on us fixing our own error.

If a payment is reversed after coins were delivered — a chargeback, or a card that turns out to be stolen — we will remove those coins. If they have already been spent, the balance can go negative and must be settled before you can buy again.

You close it: withdraw your wallet balance first. Unspent coins are not refunded on closure, because they were bought to be used here. Tell us before you close if there is a balance you cannot withdraw and we will sort it out.

We close it for breaking the rules: you can still withdraw money already earned, unless it came from the fraud that got the account closed. Coins are forfeited.

If TrustFirst shuts down

Then we would give reasonable notice, stop selling coins, and let you withdraw your wallet balance whether or not it reaches the usual R50 minimum. We would rather say that now, while it costs nothing to promise, than leave you guessing.

7. What happens when a rule is broken

What we can do

Not everything is a ban, and treating a first rude comment the same as a scam would be its own kind of unfairness. Depending on what happened we may:

  • Put it behind a tap rather than remove it, where it is graphic rather than forbidden.
  • Show it to fewer people.
  • Take away a feature for a while — going live, sending messages, posting.
  • Remove a verification badge.
  • Suspend the account for a period.
  • Close it permanently.

We try to pick the smallest thing that fixes the problem.

How something reaches us

Three ways: somebody reports it, an automatic check flags an image, or we come across it ourselves. Most of it is the first, which is why the report button matters more than it looks.

Being honest about the size of this: TrustFirst is reviewed by a very small team. We look at reports as quickly as we can and we will not promise a time we cannot keep. Anything involving a child, a credible threat, or somebody in danger goes to the front of the queue ahead of everything else.

Being told what happened

If we act against your account we will tell you what we did, what it was about, and how to challenge it. Enforcement you have to guess at is not enforcement, it is just things breaking.

The exception is where telling you would put somebody at risk, or where the law says we may not.

Disagreeing with us

Every decision can be challenged. Open a support request in the app, or write to contacttrustfirst@gmail.com, and say why you think we got it wrong.

A different look is a real look. If we were wrong we will undo it, put the content back, and say so rather than quietly reversing it and hoping you do not notice.

When we act first and talk afterwards

For a small set of things we remove or suspend immediately and work it out after: anything involving a child, a threat against a specific person, intimate images posted without consent, an account that has been taken over, or fraud in progress.

You can still appeal. It just happens after the thing has stopped rather than before.

Doing it again

We keep a record of what we have done to an account and why, so that a pattern is visible as a pattern. Repetition moves you up the list above. One mistake is a mistake; the same thing three times is a decision.

Making a new account to get around a ban

If we have suspended or closed your account, do not make another one to carry on. Doing so is itself a reason to close the new one, and it turns a temporary suspension into a permanent one.

This is the one place where having several accounts, which is otherwise perfectly allowed, becomes a problem.

Your content and your money while this is happening

A suspension hides your profile and posts from other people; it does not delete them. If the suspension is lifted they come back. If the account is closed permanently, your content goes.

Money is handled separately and deliberately so: see your wallet. Being suspended does not forfeit what you have already earned, unless the earnings are themselves the fraud.

Behaviour somewhere else

We generally do not police what you do off TrustFirst. The exception is narrow and serious: where something elsewhere means you are a real danger to people here, such as using another platform to organise harm to someone on this one.

Courts and the police

We will remove content and hand over information when South African law requires us to, and we will tell you when that has happened to your account unless we are forbidden from saying.

What we cannot hand over is the contents of your direct messages. They are end-to-end encrypted and we do not hold a readable copy, so there is nothing to give. That is a property of how the app is built rather than a position we take case by case.

We will not always get it right

Moderation at any scale involves judgement calls made quickly with incomplete information, and some of ours will be wrong. We aim to be consistent, and the fact that something similar was missed elsewhere does not mean a decision about you was wrong — it more likely means we have not seen the other one yet.

Tell us when we get it wrong. That is what the appeal is for.

8. Verification and badges

What a badge actually means

It means we checked something specific and it held up. Nothing more than that, and the colour tells you which thing:

  • Blue, Verified — a real person, confirmed by a face check or an identity document.
  • Silver, Business — a real business, confirmed by registration papers or a company email address.
  • Red, Official — a government body or official, confirmed by an official email address or a letter of employment, and always read by a person.
  • Green, Protected — a child account, linked to a verified parent or guardian.
  • Gold, Public Figure — granted by us, not applied for.
What it does not mean

A badge is not us vouching for someone. It is not a mark of quality, honesty or safety, and it is not our endorsement of anything they post.

A verified account can still lie to you, and a verified business can still be one you should not buy from. The badge tells you they are who they say they are. Whether they are worth trusting is still yours to judge.

What verification unlocks

You need to be verified to start a conversation with someone new. That is the only thing gated behind it. It exists because opening a direct message to a stranger is how most scams and harassment begin, and making that step cost something stops most of it.

Everything else — posting, clips, comments, going live, replying to messages already open — works without a badge.

What happens to the document you send

It goes into private storage only a reviewer can open, and it is deleted the moment your application is decided, whichever way it goes. If nobody reviews it within 7 days it is deleted anyway. We keep the outcome, never the document or your ID number.

Cover your ID number before photographing it. A reviewer needs your face, your name and the type of document, and nothing else. The full account of this is in the Privacy Policy.

Saying no, and taking one back

We can refuse an application, and we do not have to explain the reasoning in detail — saying exactly what failed a check is a manual for getting past it next time.

We can also remove a badge later: if it was obtained with false documents, if the account is handed to somebody else, if a business closes or an official leaves the post it was granted for, or if the account is used to abuse the trust the badge creates.

Faking one

Do not put a tick in your display name or profile picture, and do not claim to be verified when you are not. It is treated as impersonation, and the account is closed.

Badges cannot be bought, and nobody offering to sell you one is able to deliver it.

9. Messages, and what encryption really does

What end-to-end encrypted means

Your direct messages are scrambled on your device before they leave it, and unscrambled on the other person's device when they arrive. The keys never leave those two devices.

What sits on our servers in between is the scrambled version. We cannot turn it back into words, and neither can anyone who steals it from us.

What we can still see

Encryption hides what you said. It does not hide that you said it. We can see that an account messaged another account, roughly when, and how big the message was. We need that much to deliver it at all.

So: private conversation, visible correspondence. If the fact of talking to someone is itself sensitive, know that this is the part encryption does not cover.

We cannot get your messages back

This is the cost of the promise above. Lose your device, clear your browser data, or lose the account, and the messages on it are gone. There is no copy we are holding back, no support request that recovers them, and no amount of proving who you are that helps.

If a conversation matters to you, keep your own copy of it.

Encrypted for another device

Sometimes a message shows as encrypted for a different device and cannot be opened. That is not a fault, it is the encryption working: it was sealed for a device that is not the one you are on, and only that device holds the key.

It usually happens after signing in somewhere new. Ask the sender to send it again and it will be sealed for the device you are actually using.

The rules apply here too

Everything in the rules applies in a private message. Private does not mean permitted.

What changes is how we find out. We cannot scan your conversations, so a report from the person receiving it is the only way a message reaches us. Reporting one sends us that message, which is the only reason we can act on it at all.

Disappearing messages

You can set a conversation so new messages disappear once seen, after 24 hours, or after 7 days. Either person can turn it on or change it, and both are told when it changes.

It removes messages from the conversation. It cannot remove them from somebody's memory, their notes, or a photograph of their screen. Treat it as tidying up, not as a guarantee.

Screenshots

Where the app can tell that a screenshot was taken, it says so in the conversation. It cannot always tell. A web browser gives an app no way of knowing, so a screenshot taken there is silent, and there are other ways to photograph a screen that nothing can detect.

So do not treat the absence of a notice as proof that nothing was captured. Anything you send to another person is, in the end, in their hands.

Who you are talking to

Encryption protects the message in transit. It says nothing about whether the person at the other end is who they claim to be, or what they will do with what you send them.

You must be verified to open a conversation with someone new, which raises the cost of pretending, but it does not eliminate it. Be as careful about what you send as you would be with anyone you have not met.

Stopping someone

Blocking is immediate and does not need us. A blocked person cannot message you, see your posts, mention you, tag you or interact with you anywhere in the app. You do not have to explain it to anyone, including them.

Voice notes, photos and video in messages

Media sent in a conversation is kept in private storage rather than anywhere public, and is not reachable by a stray link the way a posted clip is.

The same limit applies as everywhere else in this section: it is protected from everyone except the person you sent it to.

10. Eddie, and other AI features

Eddie is software, not a person

Eddie is an AI assistant. It is not a member of staff, it does not speak for us, and nothing it says is a promise from TrustFirst. If Eddie tells you how something on the app works and these Terms say otherwise, these Terms are what counts.

It will be confidently wrong sometimes

Language models invent things. Eddie can state a fact that does not exist, get a date wrong, or describe a feature this app has never had, and it will do all of that in the same certain tone it uses when it is right.

Check anything that matters. Do not use Eddie as your source for medical, legal, financial or safety decisions — it is not qualified to give any of that, and neither are we through it.

Where what you type goes

Eddie does not run on our own machines. What you send it is passed to an outside AI provider, which produces the reply and sends it back. Depending on our arrangement with that provider, what passes through may be used by them to improve their own systems.

So treat Eddie as a conversation that leaves TrustFirst, because it does. Do not paste anything into it you would not want outside this app: not passwords, not ID numbers, not somebody else's private information.

Eddie has no access to your direct messages. Those are encrypted and it cannot reach them any more than we can.

Eddie is not help in a crisis

If you are in danger or thinking of hurting yourself, Eddie is not the thing to talk to. It is not a counsellor, it cannot call anyone, and it cannot tell how serious a situation is.

11. Going live

Who can broadcast

You must be 16 to start a live stream. Anyone old enough to have an account can watch one. The reasoning is in the age limits: broadcasting yourself to strangers in real time is a different act from posting a clip you had time to think about.

There is no undo

Everything you say, show, and have in the background of your room goes out as it happens. Nobody reviews it first, and you cannot edit it afterwards, because there is no afterwards.

Look behind you before you start. Addresses, school uniforms, post with your name on it, other people who did not agree to be on camera — all of it is broadcast the moment it is in frame.

What is kept afterwards

We do not record the video. When a stream ends, it is gone from our side: there is no replay, and no copy sitting in storage. If we ever change that, we will say so before it happens rather than after.

The chat and reactions are kept, so somebody arriving late can follow the conversation. Treat a live chat as public and permanent, because that is what it is.

Bringing someone up as a guest

When somebody asks to join, you see their username and picture so you can decide. If you accept, their camera and microphone go out to everyone watching until they leave or you remove them.

You are responsible for what happens on your stream, including what a guest does on it. Choose who you bring up, and remove them the moment it goes wrong. Do not bring up somebody you cannot see the account of.

Being a guest on someone else's

Asking to join shares your username and picture with the host before anything else happens. Being accepted puts your camera and microphone in front of everyone in that room, which may be a great many people you know nothing about.

You can leave at any time, and leaving ends it immediately.

Gifts during a stream

Gifts sent while you are live work exactly as they do anywhere else: 80 percent to you, 20 percent to the platform, and final once sent. The full arrangement is in coins, gifts and your wallet.

Do not promise things in exchange for gifts that you do not intend to do, and do not ask a viewer for anything that would break the rules. Pressuring viewers to send gifts, especially any you can tell are young, is a reason to lose the ability to go live.

What you may not stream

Everything in the rules applies while you are live, and a stream that breaks them is ended rather than reviewed afterwards.

Two that catch people out in particular. Do not stream other people's work — films, series, sport, concerts, or music playing loudly enough to be the point. And do not stream people who have not agreed to be on camera, which includes strangers in public places who did not know you were filming.

Other people can record you

We do not keep the video, but a viewer can point a second phone at their screen and there is nothing we or anyone else can do about it. Anything you broadcast may end up saved and shared somewhere else entirely.

Go live as though it is being recorded, because sometimes it is.

If a stream is cut off

We may end a stream immediately where it is breaking the rules or somebody is at risk. We will tell you why, and you can appeal it as you can any other decision — see disagreeing with us.

Streams also fail for ordinary reasons: bad signal, a device running out of battery, our own service having a problem. Those are not enforcement, and we cannot promise a stream will never drop.

12. Leaving, and what happens to your things

You can leave whenever you like

Delete your account from Settings, Privacy. You do not have to ask us, give a reason, or talk to anyone first, and nothing in this agreement traps you here.

Do these two things first
  • Withdraw your wallet. Money left in a closed account is a problem to unpick afterwards. If the balance is under the R50 minimum and you cannot withdraw it, write to us before you close and we will sort it out.
  • Download your data if you want to keep it. After deletion we cannot produce it for you, because it is not there.

Unspent coins are not refunded when you close an account. They were bought to be used here.

What deletion actually removes

Your profile, posts, clips, comments and the account itself. It stops being reachable straight away, and the data goes from our systems rather than being hidden and kept.

Copies can persist briefly in caches and backups before those are cycled out. That is a matter of days, not a quiet archive.

What deletion cannot reach

Three things, and it is better to know them now than to discover them after.

  • Messages you sent to other people. Their copy is on their device, encrypted for them. We could not remove it even if we wanted to, and closing your account does not touch it.
  • Anything somebody else reposted or saved before you left.
  • Records we are required by law to keep, chiefly transactions. We keep the record of a payment, not your profile.
Your username afterwards

It is released and somebody else may take it. If you think you might come back, that is a reason to keep the account rather than delete it.

When we end it instead

We may suspend or close an account for breaking these Terms, as section 7 describes. We may also have to close one where the law requires it, or where keeping it open would put other people at risk.

We will tell you, and you can appeal. Money already earned stays yours unless the earnings are themselves the fraud that ended the account.

Accounts nobody uses

We may close an account that has gone unused for a very long time, so that storage is not held for ever for nobody. If we ever do this we will email you first and give you a fair chance to log in and stop it. We will not do it to an account holding money.

Child accounts

A parent or guardian can close a linked child account at any time, and withdrawing their consent closes it. The same deletion applies as anywhere else.

What survives the end of this agreement

Most of these Terms stop applying when you leave. A few carry on because they would be meaningless otherwise: the sections on liability, on content somebody else has already reposted, on records we must keep, and on how disputes between us are resolved.

Coming back

You are welcome to make a new account later, unless we closed the last one for breaking the rules. What you cannot do is get the old one back: deletion is final, and there is nothing to restore.
