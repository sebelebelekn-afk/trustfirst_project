2. Who may use TrustFirst

The three age limits

There is more than one, because posting a clip and spending money are not the same act.

  • 13 to have an account at all.
  • 16 to broadcast a live stream. Anyone old enough for an account may watch one.
  • 18 to buy coins, send a gift, receive a gift, or hold a wallet balance. There is no exception to this one, with or without a parent's permission.

The money limit is not us being careful for the sake of it. A person under 18 in South Africa cannot enter a binding contract on their own, so a purchase made by a minor can be reversed by their guardian afterwards. By then the gift has been sent and the creator has been paid, and the loss falls on the platform and on them.

If you are under 18

South African law treats anyone under 18 as a child, and your information may only be handled with the permission of a parent or guardian. On TrustFirst that permission is given through a child account linked to their account.

Without that link, an account for someone under 18 cannot be used. This is not a setting we can waive.

If you are the parent or guardian

By linking a child account you are agreeing to these Terms on their behalf and consenting to their information being handled as the Privacy Policy describes. You can see their activity, change what they are allowed to do, and close the account at any time.

You are responsible for what happens on that account, which is the other half of being able to control it.

Proving your age

We may ask you to confirm your age, particularly before anything involving money. If we have good reason to think an account belongs to someone younger than they said, we may restrict it until the question is answered.

If we find that someone under 18 has spent money on TrustFirst, we will refund what is left and close off the ability to spend. We may not be able to recover a gift that has already been paid out to a creator.

Accounts, and how many you may have

You may have more than one account, and the app is built to switch between them. What you may not do is pretend to be somebody else, run accounts automatically, or make accounts in order to get around a suspension.

Keep your password to yourself. Anything done from your account is treated as done by you, so tell us quickly if you think someone else is in it.

People we cannot let in

You may not use TrustFirst if we have already closed your account for breaking these Terms, or if the law where you are does not allow you to use a service like this.

3. Your content, and what we may do with it

It stays yours

You own everything you post. Posting a clip on TrustFirst does not hand it to us, does not make it ours to sell, and does not stop you putting it anywhere else. Nothing in these Terms transfers ownership of anything you made.

The permission you give us

We need your permission to show your content to other people, because that is the entire function of the app. Without it we could not put your clip in somebody's feed.

So when you post, you give us a non-exclusive, royalty-free, worldwide licence to store, copy, display and distribute that content for the purpose of running TrustFirst. In practice that means: keeping it on our servers, sending it to your followers, making a thumbnail of it, resizing it so it loads on a slow connection, and letting it be found in search inside the app.

Non-exclusive means you can still do whatever you like with it elsewhere. Royalty-free means we do not owe you a fee for showing it, which is the ordinary arrangement for a platform you are not paying to use.

The limits on that permission

This licence is deliberately narrower than the one most platforms take, and here is what it does not include:

  • We will not sell or license your content to other companies, and we will not hand it to a partner to publish somewhere else.
  • We will not put your face or your words in an advertisement. There are no adverts on TrustFirst.
  • We may pass your content to the companies that store and deliver it for us, listed in the Privacy Policy, and to nobody else. They may only handle it in order to do that job.

If we ever want to do something outside this, we have to ask you first.

When other people repost or share it

TrustFirst lets people repost a clip and share a link to it. By posting publicly you accept that this can happen, and your permission extends far enough to let it work.

You can turn some of this off per post, and where you have, we will respect it. What we cannot undo is somebody recording their screen, and we will not pretend otherwise.

What happens when you delete something

The permission above lasts as long as the content is on TrustFirst. Delete a post, or your whole account, and it ends.

Two honest caveats. Copies can sit in caches and backups for a short while before they are cleared, so deletion is quick but not instant everywhere. And if somebody else reposted it before you deleted it, or saved it to their phone, that copy is beyond our reach. Deleting removes it from TrustFirst; it does not un-share it from the world.

You have to actually have the right to post it

When you post something, you are telling us that you are allowed to: that you made it, or you have permission from whoever did, and that showing it does not break somebody else's copyright or put someone in it who did not agree to be there.

Music is where this bites most often. A song in the background of your clip belongs to whoever wrote and recorded it, and being on TrustFirst does not license it for you.

If that turns out not to be true, the consequences are yours rather than ours, and we will take the content down.

Getting paid for what you post

We do not pay you for posting. What you can earn is what other people choose to send you: when someone sends you a gift, you receive 80 percent of its value and TrustFirst keeps 20 percent to run the platform. That is the whole of the arrangement, and it is covered properly in the section on coins and gifts.

4. Other people's content, and copyright

What you see here is not ours

Almost everything on TrustFirst was made by the people using it. We did not write it, we do not vouch for it, and showing you something is not us agreeing with it.

You may come across something you find offensive or wrong. That is the nature of a place where people post freely, and while we remove what breaks the rules, we cannot promise you will never see it.

We do not check everything first

Posts go up immediately. There is no queue where a person reads each one, because at any real size there could not be. Some images are checked automatically for adult or violent content, as the Privacy Policy describes, and the rest of our moderation happens after the fact, largely because somebody reported it.

That is also the legal position: we are not obliged to monitor everything on the platform, and we do not.

Reporting something

Every post, clip, comment, message and account has a report option in its menu. Use it. It reaches us with the thing you are reporting attached, which is far more useful than a description of it.

You can also block someone, which stops them contacting you, seeing your posts, or interacting with you at all.

If somebody posted your work

Send a take-down notification to contacttrustfirst@gmail.com. South African law sets out what one has to contain, and a notice missing these cannot be acted on:

  • Your full name and address, and your signature, written or electronic.
  • Your phone number and email address.
  • Identification of the right you hold and what you say infringes it.
  • A link to the material on TrustFirst, precise enough for us to find it.
  • What you want done about it.
  • A statement that you are acting in good faith.
  • A statement that what you have told us is, to your knowledge, true and correct.

Do not send one lightly. A person who knowingly misrepresents that something infringes their rights can be held liable for the damage that causes.

What we do when we get one

We look at it, and if it holds up we remove the material. We tell the person who posted it what was removed and why, and who asked, unless we are not permitted to say.

Removing something is not us deciding who owns it. That is not ours to decide, and a court is where it goes if the two of you cannot agree.

If your post was removed and you think it should not have been

Write to us at the same address and say so, with the reason you believe you had the right to post it. We will look again. If we were wrong, we will put it back and say so.

Doing it repeatedly

An account that keeps posting other people's work after being told will be closed. One mistake is a mistake; a pattern is a decision.

Abusing the report button

Reporting is for content that breaks the rules, not for burying somebody you disagree with. Mass or bad-faith reporting is itself a reason to act against an account, and we look at who is reporting as well as what is reported.

5. Coins, gifts and your wallet

You must be 18

Everything in this section is for people over 18. Buying coins, sending a gift, receiving one, or holding a wallet balance are all closed to anyone younger, with or without a parent's permission. The reason is in the age limits.

What coins are, and what they are not

Coins are a virtual item you buy in order to send gifts. They are not money. You do not own a balance of currency with us; you hold a limited permission to use a feature of the app.

  • Coins have no cash value and cannot be turned back into money.
  • They cannot be sold, given to another person, or moved off TrustFirst.
  • They do not expire while your account is open and in good standing.
Buying coins

Prices are shown in the app before you pay, in rand, and they are what you will be charged. Coins arrive immediately once the payment goes through.

Payment is handled by Stripe or Paystack, not by us. We never see your card number. Their terms apply to the payment itself alongside ours.

We may change coin prices. A change never affects coins you have already bought.

Sending a gift

Sending a gift spends the coins. The creator receives 80 percent of its value into their wallet and TrustFirst keeps 20 percent, which is how the platform is paid for.

A gift is final. It cannot be taken back, and the coins are gone once it is sent. Send them because you want to, not expecting anything in return: a gift buys you nothing, obliges the creator to nothing, and promises you nothing.

Your wallet

Unlike coins, a wallet balance is real money, and it is yours. It is kept separate from coins and shown separately in the app.

  • The minimum withdrawal is R50.
  • Bank transfers take 1 to 3 business days once approved.
  • The bank details must be yours. We do not pay a wallet out to somebody else's account.

We hold that money for you until you withdraw it. We do not pay interest on it, and it is not a savings or investment account.

Refunds

Unused coins: if you change your mind, tell us within 7 days of buying and we will refund coins you have not spent. That is your cooling-off right for something bought electronically, and we would rather honour it than argue about it.

Spent coins: cannot be refunded. The value has already gone to a creator, and taking it back would mean taking it from them.

If something went wrong: a payment taken twice, coins that never arrived, a gift the app sent by mistake. Write to contacttrustfirst@gmail.com and we will put it right. This is separate from changing your mind, and there is no time limit on us fixing our own error.

Reversed payments

If a payment is reversed after coins were delivered — a chargeback, or a card that turns out to be stolen — we will remove those coins. If they have already been spent, the balance can go negative and must be settled before you can buy again.

Doing this deliberately is fraud, and the account will be closed.

Tax

Money you earn from gifts may be income, and declaring it is yours to do. We do not deduct tax for you and we cannot advise you on it. If you are earning meaningfully, speak to someone who does this properly.

When we hold a payout

We may pause a withdrawal while we look into it if there are signs of fraud, stolen cards, or gifts being cycled between accounts to move money about. We will tell you it is happening and why, and release it as soon as we are satisfied.

We will not hold your money because we disagree with what you post. Enforcement against an account and holding earned money are separate things.

If your account closes

You close it: withdraw your wallet balance first. Unspent coins are not refunded on closure, because they were bought to be used here. Tell us before you close if there is a balance you cannot withdraw and we will sort it out.

We close it for breaking the rules: you can still withdraw money already earned, unless it came from the fraud that got the account closed. Coins are forfeited.

If TrustFirst shuts down

Then we would give reasonable notice, stop selling coins, and let you withdraw your wallet balance whether or not it reaches the usual R50 minimum. We would rather say that now, while it costs nothing to promise, than leave you guessing.
